A sowing calendar tells you when to start vegetables and herbs indoors and when to sow them directly outdoors. In Central Europe the season begins with chillies and peppers in February, tomatoes follow from mid-March, and warmth-loving crops like beans, cucumbers and basil only go outside after the last frost in mid-May. Hardy crops such as radishes, spinach and peas, on the other hand, can be sown outdoors from March.

## Starting indoors or sowing direct?

Before you begin, it helps to understand the two ways of sowing:

### Starting seeds indoors

Plants with a long growing period or high warmth needs are started on a windowsill, in a greenhouse or under a grow light. This applies especially to tomatoes, peppers, chillies, aubergines, celery and leeks. Cabbages, lettuce, cucumbers and squash also benefit from a head start.

Keys to success:

- **Plenty of light:** Without enough light, seedlings get leggy: long, thin and pale. A south-facing window or a grow light helps.
- **The right germination temperature:** Tomatoes, peppers and chillies germinate best at around 20 to 25 °C.
- **Low-nutrient seed compost:** Prick out seedlings once they have their first pair of true leaves.
- **Hardening off:** Before planting out, put young plants outside during the day for one to two weeks and bring them in at night.

### Direct sowing

Many vegetables dislike being transplanted or grow so quickly that starting them indoors is not worth it. Carrots, radishes, parsnips, beetroot, peas, beans, spinach and dill are therefore sown straight into the bed. The soil should be dry enough to work and a little warmer; in cold, wet soil many seeds germinate poorly or rot.

## Last frost: the key date in the gardening year

In German-speaking countries the traditional marker is the **Ice Saints** (Eisheilige), the saints' days from **11 to 15 May**. According to old farming lore, night frost is possible until then. While this is not true every year and late frosts vary by region, mid-May is a good guide for Central Europe: only then do frost-tender plants go outside. These include tomatoes, peppers, chillies, aubergines, cucumbers, courgettes, squash, beans and basil.

In exposed or upland areas, cold nights can come even later. Check the forecast and keep fleece handy. If you garden in a different climate, use your local average last frost date instead.

## Month by month: what to do now

### January and February

The season starts indoors. Chillies need the longest and can be sown from late January, sweet peppers and aubergines from February. Celery and leeks are started now too. In mild weather, the first broad beans can go outside at the end of February.

### March

Now things get going. From mid-March, sow **tomatoes** indoors, along with kohlrabi, cabbages, lettuce and basil. Outdoors, once the soil has dried out, sow radishes, spinach, peas, carrots and parsley. Fleece protects against cold nights. Onion sets go in from late March.

### April

Start cucumbers, courgettes and squash indoors from mid-April; sown too early, they get too big before mid-May. Outdoors, sow beetroot, chard, more carrots, dill and lettuce. Kohlrabi and lettuce raised indoors can be planted out once hardened off.

### May

After mid-May it is planting time: tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers, courgettes, squash and basil go outside. Sow beans directly now, as they need warm soil. Cucumbers and courgettes can also be sown direct.

### June

Time for succession sowings: bush beans, carrots for storage, lettuce, kohlrabi and beetroot. From late June, sow endive, sugarloaf chicory and Chinese cabbage for autumn harvests.

### July

Last bush beans until early July, plus fennel, radicchio and autumn lettuces. Plant out kale and other winter vegetables. Sow parsley again for winter.

### August

The month for winter crops: **lamb's lettuce**, spinach, radishes and winter purslane. Sow green manure such as phacelia or mustard on empty beds.

### September to December

In September, sow more lamb's lettuce and winter lettuce. Plant garlic and overwintering onions from September to October. After that, it is planning time: sort your seeds, test germination and draw up next year's growing plan.

## Tips for successful sowing

1. **Mind the sowing depth:** As a rule of thumb, sow about two to three times as deep as the seed is thick. Light germinators like lettuce, dill and basil are only pressed in or barely covered.
2. **Keep evenly moist:** Germinating seeds must never dry out. See our [watering schedule guide](/en/blog/watering-schedule/).
3. **Plan successions:** Sow radishes, lettuce and beans in small batches every two to three weeks rather than all at once.
4. **Think about neighbours:** Plan what will grow side by side when you sow. Our guide to [companion planting](/en/blog/companion-planting/) helps.
5. **Store seeds properly:** Kept cool, dry and dark, many seeds stay viable for several years. Buy parsnip and chive seed fresh where possible.
