Many popular garden and house plants are toxic to cats and dogs, including lilies, yew, cherry laurel, thuja, ivy, oleander, dieffenbachia and the green parts of tomato plants. Lilies are especially dangerous for cats, and grapes for dogs. If your pet has eaten a toxic plant or shows symptoms, contact a vet or an emergency veterinary service immediately.

**Important:** This guide does not replace veterinary advice. How severely a plant affects an animal depends on species, body weight, the amount eaten and which part of the plant. When in doubt, it is always better to call the vet once too often.

## Why cats and dogs chew on plants

Dogs chew sticks, leaves and bulbs out of curiosity, boredom or during play, and puppies are particularly at risk. Cats eat grass and plants partly to help bring up hairballs, and indoor cats without cat grass often turn to house plants instead. Cats also ingest pollen and plant sap when grooming their fur.

## Common toxic plants at a glance

| Plant | Dangerous for | Toxic parts | Possible symptoms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lilies (Lilium), daylilies (Hemerocallis) | cats in particular | all parts, including pollen and vase water | vomiting, loss of appetite, lethargy, acute kidney failure |
| Yew (Taxus) | dogs, cats | almost all parts except the red aril; seeds highly toxic | vomiting, tremors, abnormal heart rhythm, collapse |
| Cherry laurel (Prunus laurocerasus) | dogs, cats | leaves, seeds | drooling, vomiting, breathing difficulty |
| Thuja (arborvitae) | dogs, cats | all parts, especially shoot tips | digestive upset, seizures in larger amounts |
| Ivy (Hedera helix) | dogs, cats | leaves, berries | vomiting, diarrhoea, drooling |
| Oleander | dogs, cats | all parts | vomiting, abnormal heart rhythm |
| Tomato (green parts) | dogs, cats | leaves, stems, unripe fruit | digestive upset, weakness |
| Grapes, raisins | dogs in particular | fruit | vomiting, lethargy, kidney failure |
| Onion, garlic, leek, chives | dogs, cats | all parts, raw or cooked | anaemia, weakness, pale gums |
| Dieffenbachia, monstera, philodendron | dogs, cats | leaves, stems | burning mouth, drooling, difficulty swallowing |
| Daffodil, tulip, hyacinth | dogs, cats | mainly bulbs | vomiting, diarrhoea; daffodils can also affect the heart |
| Lily of the valley, foxglove | dogs, cats | all parts | vomiting, abnormal heart rhythm |
| Rhododendron, azalea | dogs, cats | all parts | vomiting, drooling, weakness, heart problems |
| Sago palm (Cycas) | dogs in particular | all parts, especially seeds | vomiting, liver damage |

This list is not exhaustive. Boxwood, hellebore, angel's trumpet, laburnum, poinsettia and aloe vera can also cause problems.

## The most dangerous cases in detail

### Lilies and cats

True lilies and daylilies are especially dangerous for cats. Nibbling a single leaf, licking pollen off their fur or drinking water from a vase can lead to acute kidney failure. Early signs such as vomiting and lethargy often appear within a few hours, with kidney damage developing afterwards. Fast treatment is critical. If you have cats, it is best to keep lilies out of the house altogether, including in bouquets.

### Grapes and dogs

Grapes and raisins can cause kidney failure in dogs. The dangerous amount cannot be predicted, and some dogs react to small quantities. That is why any ingestion should be treated as a veterinary emergency.

### Yew

Yew is a popular hedging plant and highly toxic to dogs, cats and many other animals. Needles, wood and seeds contain taxines, which affect the heart. Only the red fleshy aril is non-toxic; the seed inside is not. Keep clippings away from animals too.

### Cherry laurel and thuja

Cherry laurel leaves and seeds contain compounds that can release cyanide during digestion. Thuja contains essential oils such as thujone. Both are common hedging plants. Dogs that like chewing twigs in particular should not play unsupervised near freshly cut hedges.

### Tomatoes and the vegetable garden

The green parts of tomato plants, meaning leaves, stems and unripe fruit, contain glycoalkaloids such as solanine and tomatine. Ripe red tomatoes in small amounts are generally considered unproblematic. Raw green potatoes and potato sprouts are also toxic. Onion-family plants such as onions, garlic, leeks and chives can damage red blood cells in dogs and cats.

## Signs of poisoning

Symptoms can appear immediately or only hours to days later. Common warning signs include:

- heavy drooling, pawing at the mouth, difficulty swallowing
- vomiting and diarrhoea, possibly with blood
- lethargy, weakness, unsteadiness
- tremors or seizures
- noticeably slow, fast or irregular heartbeat
- breathing difficulty
- pale or yellowish gums
- reduced urination or none at all

## What to do if you suspect poisoning

1. **Stay calm** and keep your pet away from the plant. Remove plant material from the mouth if you can do so safely.
2. **Call your vet or an emergency vet immediately.** Explain which plant, which part, how much and when.
3. **Bring the plant:** Take a piece of it or a photo, and if your pet has vomited, a sample if possible.
4. **Do not induce vomiting yourself** or give home remedies such as salt water or milk. This can do more harm than good.
5. **Do not wait and see**, even if your pet seems fine. With lilies, grapes or yew, every hour counts.

## Making your home and garden pet-safe

Prevention is easier than treatment. These steps have proven useful:

- **Take stock:** Walk through your home, balcony and garden once and identify every plant, ideally by its botanical name.
- **Relocate toxic plants:** Move house plants such as dieffenbachia or poinsettia to rooms your pet cannot access, or give them away. Hanging baskets are not a safe solution for cats, as they climb well.
- **Clear away clippings promptly:** Do not leave yew, thuja or cherry laurel prunings lying around. Freshly cut twigs are a favourite toy for many dogs.
- **Store bulbs safely:** Do not leave tulip and daffodil bulbs out in the shed or on the patio in autumn; dogs also like to dig them up.
- **Fence off the vegetable patch:** A low fence keeps dogs away from tomatoes, potatoes and onions.
- **Offer alternatives:** Cat grass and chew toys distract from plants.

## Safe alternatives for home and garden

Having pets does not mean giving up on plants. The following are generally considered non-toxic to cats and dogs:

- **House plants:** spider plant, areca palm, Boston fern, calathea, Chinese money plant (Pilea), African violet
- **For cats:** cat grass (such as wheat or oat grass) and catnip for enrichment
- **Herbs:** basil, rosemary, thyme and lemon balm
- **Garden:** sunflowers, calendula, nasturtium, and as hedging alternatives, for example hornbeam or serviceberry

"Non-toxic" does not mean a plant is fine in large amounts; even harmless plants can cause stomach upset. When unsure, check the exact botanical species, as common names are often ambiguous.
